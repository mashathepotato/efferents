# Window length sets an interior optimum for tipping-point early-warning lead time

## Response to reviewers (round 1)

- Ceiling-normalized lead column added to Results (critical §1, neutral);
  the novelty claim now leads with the ceiling constraint (enthusiast).
- Detection rate and conditional lead split from the miss-as-zero mean
  (critical §3, neutral).
- Calibration sample size and the small-window fragility moved into Results
  (critical §2).
No new experiments were run; all numbers derive from the recorded runs.

## Motivation

Tipping elements exhibit critical slowing down — rising lag-1 autocorrelation
(AC1) — ahead of transitions, but an operational alarm must choose an
estimation window: short windows are noisy, long windows lag the drift.
Practitioners rarely publish this tradeoff curve. Hypothesis (falsifier: no
window setting beats the base configuration's lead time at fixed ~5%
series-level false-alarm rate): maximize mean detection lead time by tuning
the rolling-window length.

## Methods

Deterministic AR(1) ensemble (seed 20260721): 20 control series (phi=0.5) and
20 transitioning series whose phi ramps 0.5→0.98 over t∈[100,400) with
transition at T_C=400, length 500, noise sd 1.0. Detector: rolling AC1 over
window w; alarm threshold = 95th percentile of per-series maxima of rolling
AC1 on 10 training control series (pins series-level false-alarm rate near
5%); alarm fires on 2 consecutive threshold crossings, so the earliest
possible alarm is t=w+1 and **max achievable lead = T_C − w − 1**. Score on
10 held-out transitioning series; report the held-out control false-alarm
rate alongside. Sweep w ∈ {10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300, 380}. Generator and
detector fully specified in `datagen.py`, `train.py`, `eval.py` at
`code_sha`; the prose above suffices to reimplement.

## Results

| run | w | mean lead | ceiling (T_C−w−1) | lead/ceiling | detected | lead over detections |
|-----|---|-----------|-------------------|--------------|----------|----------------------|
| run_00 | 10 | 32.6 | 389 | 0.08 | 0.4 | 81.5 |
| run_01 | 25 | 66.4 | 374 | 0.18 | 0.7 | 94.9 |
| run_02 | 50 | 74.8 | 349 | 0.21 | 1.0 | 74.8 |
| run_03 | 100 | 78.5 | 299 | 0.26 | 1.0 | 78.5 |
| run_04 | 200 | 97.5 | 199 | 0.49 | 1.0 | 97.5 |
| run_05 | 300 | 77.7 | 99 | 0.78 | 1.0 | 77.7 |
| run_06 | 380 | 18.5 | 19 | 0.97 | 1.0 | 18.5 |

The two failure modes separate cleanly. Left side: detection itself is
fragile — w=10 detects 40% of transitions and its conditional lead (81.5) is
already high, so the miss-as-zero mean understates earliness; calibration
there rests on 10 control series and shows a 0.1 held-out false-alarm rate
(`run_00`). Right side: detection is perfect and the detector saturates its
ceiling — at w=380 it achieves 97% of the 19-step maximum. The raw-lead
optimum (97.5 at w=200, `run_04`; false-alarm rate 0.0) sits where the
rising ceiling-utilization curve crosses the falling ceiling itself.

## Conclusion

The falsifier fails: w=200 beats the base configuration by +30.3% (97.5 vs
74.8). The right side of the tradeoff is mechanical, not statistical: any
windowed indicator inherits max lead = T_C − w − 1, and window choices near
the record length measure the ceiling, not the signal. Single-ensemble
scope (seed 20260721) is a stated limitation; the recipe makes the claim
mechanically checkable.
